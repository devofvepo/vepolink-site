The measurement.
The method.

Pulse-echo level measurement, converted to flow

A transducer mounted above the liquid sends an ultrasonic pulse and times its returning echo. This gives the distance to the surface and the liquid level. At a flume or weir, the configured head–discharge relationship converts the measured head into flow.

The transducer measures from above the channel without contacting the liquid.

A correctly specified primary device, such as a Parshall flume or a V-notch or rectangular weir, provides the relationship between head and flow.

Local indication and the selected plant interface carry level, calculated flow and totalised volume. False-echo suppression helps distinguish the surface from fixed obstructions.

Typical applications

  • Wastewater inlet and outlet channels
  • Effluent discharge
  • Irrigation channels
  • Stormwater measurement
  • Flumes and weirs

Technical specifications.

The range, resolution, repeatability and accuracy below describe level measurement. Flow range and accuracy depend on the flume or weir, its geometry, condition and configured head–discharge relationship.

Plan the installation.
Keep the reading dependable.

Flow range and accuracy depend on the flume or weir, its geometry and the configured head–discharge curve. The level range is not a flow range.

Mounting and integration

Mount vertically with the required beam clearance at the primary device’s specified head-measurement point. Confirm the flume or weir geometry, level reference and flow conversion before use.

Inspection and verification

Keep the transducer face and acoustic path clear. Check the measured level against a reference, inspect the flume or weir for deposits or damage, and verify the head–discharge settings. Foam, vapour and obstructions require a signal check under actual operating conditions.

Connect the selected output to compatible receiving hardware, with the correct power, scaling and protocol configuration.
