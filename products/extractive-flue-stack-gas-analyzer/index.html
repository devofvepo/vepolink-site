01 · Overview

Continuous emissions, kept intact.

Flue gas is hot, wet, acidic and dusty. The extractive system keeps the sample above its dew point from probe to cell, filters it in two stages, and measures it inside a clean, conditioned cabinet instead of at the stack.

NDUV with partial least squares resolves SO₂ and NOx down to 0.1 ppm. NDIR, FTIR, TDLAS, DOAS and zirconia O₂ are configured per gas, range and reporting requirement.

02 · Working principle

Extract hot, measure clean.

The system preserves the sample first, then chooses the right optical or electrochemical technique for each reported gas.

Sampling

Heated path, two-stage filter

Probe · heated line · secondary filtration

A heated probe, heated line and secondary filter protect the sample and analyzer from dust, condensation and soluble-gas loss.

NDUV + PLS

Full spectrum, not one band

Pulsed xenon · diode array · 0.1 ppm

Pulsed UV light crosses the sample cell onto a high-resolution diode array; PLS separates overlapping absorption structure for each species.

Zirconia

Oxygen reference

O₂ correction to reference conditions

The zirconia cell compares sample oxygen against reference air, supporting combustion control and correction to the required reference O₂ level.

Calibration

Automatic and remote

Zero gas · certified span gas · pressure comp.

Solenoids introduce zero and certified span gas on a timed cycle; every check is logged while pressure compensation keeps readings stable.

05 · Installation & upkeep

Installed as a system, not a box.

A CEMS is only as reliable as its sampling path: representative siting, heat from probe to cell and documented zero/span checks.

01 · Plane

Representative section

Choose a straight stack section with developed flow, clear of bends, dampers and dilution.

02 · Probe

Heated, filtered, purged

Use a heated coarse filter and automatic back purge to shed dust without stopping measurement.

03 · Line

Heated to 200 °C

Keep the transport path above water and acid dew points so soluble gases arrive intact.

04 · Shelter

Conditioned cabinet

Place analyzer, pump, filters and calibration gas in an IP65 enclosure away from stack heat and vibration.

05 · Calibration

Automatic and remote

Timed zero/span checks with certified gas are adjustable, logged and reviewable remotely.

06 · Service

Modular swap

Maintain sampling filters and replace modules, lamp and calibration gases on their planned service cycle.

Extractive versus in-situ

Extractive systems put the optics in a clean, temperature-controlled cabinet where calibration gas can be applied through the whole path. Cross-duct TDLAS is the counterpart for single-component hostile ducts.

Data and reporting

Report corrected concentrations with dust, flow, humidity and temperature through SCADA or DAS, creating a complete emission record for operations and compliance.

07 · FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is an extractive stack gas analyzer?

An online CEMS that continuously draws stack gas into a conditioned cabinet for measurement.

Which gases can be monitored?

SO₂, NOx, CO, CO₂, O₂, NH₃, HCl, HF and Cl₂, plus dust, humidity, temperature and flow as required.

Is an NO₂ converter required?

No. NO and NO₂ are measured directly, avoiding converter efficiency loss that can bias NOx.

Why is the sampling system heated?

It prevents condensation that could absorb SO₂, HCl and NH₃ and understate the true emission.

How is the probe kept clear?

Automatic back purge clears the probe filter on a schedule, while secondary filtration protects the downstream module.

Does it support remote calibration?

Yes. Automatic and remote zero/span cycles use certified gases and log every check.

Can it integrate with SCADA?

Yes, via RS-232, RS-485, 4-20 mA and local USB download.

How stable is the measurement?

Accuracy, linearity, zero drift and span drift are each specified at 2%, supported by a pulsed xenon source and no moving optics.
