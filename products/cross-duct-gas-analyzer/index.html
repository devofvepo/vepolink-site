01 · Overview

The sample never leaves the duct.

Extractive analyzers must transport and condition the sample through heated lines, filters and coolers. A cross duct analyzer removes that chain. The laser passes through the process stream, the gas absorbs its specific wavelength and concentration is known in situ in about one second.

The system combines a transmitter, receiver and connection box. The transmitter scans a tunable diode laser, the receiver performs photoelectric conversion and spectral analysis, and the connection box carries power, compensation inputs and plant outputs.

02 · Measurement principle

Light in, concentration out.

TDLAS measures how much light the target gas removes at one precise wavelength. The system stabilises that measurement in a hot, dusty and moving duct.

TDLAS

Absorption across the duct

Diode laser · optical path up to 20 m

Target molecules absorb part of the beam at the line centre. Attenuation is proportional to concentration and optical path length, giving the process value directly.

Selectivity

One line, no interference

Single-line spectrum technology

Scanning one isolated absorption line rather than a broad band minimises bias from water, carbon dioxide and other background species in raw flue gas.

System

Transmitter, receiver, box

Photoelectric conversion · spectral analysis

The transmitter launches and aligns the beam, the receiver converts and analyses it, and the flameproof connection box provides convenient plant interfaces.

Protection

Flameproof and purged

Ex d IIC T6 Gb · IP66 · 0.3-0.8 MPa

Industrial nitrogen or purified instrument air keeps the optical windows clean and cool while the integrated flameproof enclosure supports hazardous-area installation.

05 · Installation & upkeep

Aligned once, left to work.

Representative flow, opposed flanges, stable alignment and a steady purge determine measurement quality and keep service to two planned visits a year.

01 · Location

Representative section

Choose a straight, well-mixed duct section away from bends, dampers, leakage and air ingress.

02 · Flanges

Opposed pair

Weld matching flanges concentrically on the optical axis to carry the transmitter and receiver.

03 · Alignment

XY adjustment

Centre the beam on the receiver aperture and use signal level to confirm the optical path.

04 · Purge

N₂ or instrument air

Maintain 0.3-0.8 MPa clean purge gas to protect the windows from dust and condensate.

05 · Wiring

Connection box

Bring in 24 V DC and wire analog, serial, GPRS and relay interfaces to the plant system.

06 · Service

Twice a year

Clean the optical windows and calibrate. There is no sample line, filter or cooler to maintain.

In-situ versus extractive

An extractive system must pull gas out, keep it hot, filter it and deliver it unchanged. The cross duct analyzer measures in place, removing common adsorption, blockage and sample-damage failure modes.

Compensation and reporting

Temperature and pressure transmitters feed the two analog inputs so concentration is corrected to the required reporting basis. Results go to DCS, PLC, data systems or a remote server.

07 · FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is the Cross Duct Gas Analyzer?

It is an industrial TDLAS analyzer for direct in-situ gas measurement across ducts and large process streams.

Which gases can it measure?

Configurations are available for O₂, CO, NH₃, CO₂, CH₄, H₂O, HCl and HF, selected for the process at order.

Does it need sample pretreatment?

No. The measurement is made inside the duct, avoiding heated sample lines, filters, coolers and associated sample damage.

What is the response time?

Approximately one second, fast enough for combustion, reagent dosing and process-control loops.

How long an optical path is supported?

Up to 20 m, covering most ducts and stacks in power, cement, steel and chemical plants.

Is it suitable for hazardous areas?

Yes. The integrated structure is rated Ex d IIC T6 Gb with IP66 enclosure protection.

What purge gas is required?

Industrial nitrogen or purified instrument air at 0.3-0.8 MPa keeps the windows clean and cool.

What outputs are available?

Two 4-20 mA outputs, two compensation inputs, RS-485, RS-232, GPRS and two 24 V 1 A relays.
