The measurement.
The method.

Pulse radar time-of-flight

The sensor sends short radar pulses toward the water and measures the return time. A configured reference converts the measured distance into water level without putting the sensor in the water.

A 0.4–35 m distance range suits elevated mounting points and remote water-level monitoring.

Low resting power supports duty-cycled field systems; size the complete sensor, logger and telemetry power budget.

Select SDI-12, RS-485 with SDI-12 commands or a scaled 4–20 mA signal for the receiving system.

Typical applications

  • Streams and rivers
  • Tidal monitoring
  • Reservoirs and lakes
  • Flood warning
  • Bridges and culverts

Technical specifications.

Accuracy varies across three distance bands; the ±3 mm figure applies only from 2–30 m. Confirm the selected configuration, site geometry, power and interface before order.

Plan the installation.
Keep the reading dependable.

Water level depends on the mounting datum and a clear path to the actual surface.

Mounting and integration

Mount above the highest expected water level while keeping the surface at least 0.4 m from the sensor. Keep the 12° beam clear of supports, banks and obstructions. Set the datum and, where used, the 4–20 mA scaling at commissioning. Confirm the cable route and receiving interface.

Inspection and verification

Inspect mounting, enclosure, cable and beam path. Check the level reading against a known reference and review the site after flood debris or structural changes. A non-contact installation avoids routine cleaning of a wetted sensor face, but does not remove the need for inspection.

Confirm the exact output, protocol and supply against the receiving hardware. Connect to Vepolink logging and monitoring through a suitable configuration.
