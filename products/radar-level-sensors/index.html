Plan the mounting.
Protect the reading.

Keep the sensor above the highest expected water level, with a clear beam to the surface and access for inspection.

Datum and range

Record the sensor elevation and expected water levels. PulseRadar has a 0.4 m near-range limit; Smart and HF brochures state no measurement blanking distance, but physical clearance is still needed.

Beam and mounting

Check the beam against banks, bridge members, vegetation and flood debris. Mount securely and commission the orientation at the actual site.

Interfaces and upkeep

Confirm power, protocol, cable run and logger capacity. Inspect the housing and signal path periodically and verify readings against a reference.

Before you
specify a sensor.

Does a radar sensor measure flow?

These sensors measure distance to the water surface and report water level after a datum is configured. A discharge value requires a site-specific rating relationship; a level reading alone is not a flow measurement.

Which model suits a solar-powered site?

PulseRadar has the lowest stated resting power and a 0.4–35 m range. Size the full sensor, logger and telemetry power budget for the intended interval before selection.

Do these sensors work with an existing logger?

Match the exact protocol, electrical interface and power requirements. PulseRadar RS-485 uses SDI-12 commands; RadarSense Smart and HF list RS-485 Modbus RTU. A matching connector or RS-485 label alone does not establish compatibility.

Can a level sensor stay underwater?

These are non-contact sensors designed to measure from above the water. Smart and HF brochures state temporary flood tolerance for the lower housing, not continuous underwater use. Confirm site flood level, mounting and enclosure needs.
