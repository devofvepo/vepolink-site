01 · Overview

Benzene and its neighbours, speciated by name.

A gas chromatograph with a built-in pre-concentration system. Ambient hydrocarbons enrich on a Tenax GR trap, desorb thermally and separate on a capillary column before a photo-ionization detector reads each compound by retention time.

An industrial x86 PC with a full-colour touchscreen runs every analysis and stores each chromatogram to disk. RS-232, analog and digital I/O, and TCP/IP outputs carry named compound results to station, SCADA and regulatory systems.

02 · Measurement principle

How chromatography separates BTEX.

Ambient hydrocarbons arrive as a trace mixture. Enrichment, thermal desorption, capillary separation and compound-sensitive detection turn that mixture into a named, quantified list every cycle.

Pre-concentration

Tenax GR Trap

Sorbent trap · controlled sample volume

Sample air is drawn through a Tenax GR sorbent trap. Hydrocarbons adsorb and enrich on the trap while the bulk of the air passes through and vents.

Trace levels, enriched

Separation

Thermal Desorption & Column

Flash heating · capillary column

The trap is flash-heated; desorbed hydrocarbons sweep onto a capillary column and separate by retention time into resolved, named peaks.

One mixture, many peaks

Detection

Photo-Ionization Detector

UV ionization · ion current

Eluting compounds are ionized by UV light. The resulting current is proportional to concentration, with high sensitivity to benzene and other aromatic hydrocarbons.

Ion current ∝ concentration

Data path

Chromatogram & Reporting

RS-232 · TCP/IP · analog / digital

Every chromatogram is saved to disk and identified by retention time, then reported over network, modem or serial connection for review and audit.

Reviewable, exportable data

03 · Analysis cycle

One 30-minute run, start to result.

Each cycle repeats the same four stages automatically, producing a fresh chromatogram and a named concentration for every configured compound.

  1. 01

    Trap loading

    Ambient air moves through the Tenax GR trap at a controlled volume; hydrocarbons adsorb as bulk air vents.

  2. 02

    Thermal desorption

    The trap flash-heats and nitrogen carrier gas sweeps the enriched hydrocarbons onto the capillary column.

  3. 03

    Column separation

    Benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene and xylene isomers elute individually according to retention time.

  4. 04

    Detect and report

    The PID measures each peak, identifies the compound, and stores and transmits the full chromatogram.

Why speciation matters

Not total VOC. A named answer.

BTEX measurements separate individual aromatic hydrocarbons, so a station can distinguish regulatory benzene exposure and source patterns that a single total-hydrocarbon value would hide.

Why pre-concentration matters

Ambient benzene can run from under 0.1 to over 100 µg/m³. Enriching the sample before desorption makes a 0.03 ppbV lower detectable limit practical in the field.

Why benzene is monitored

Benzene is a known carcinogen. CPCB makes ambient measurement obligatory and specifies an annual mean limit under 5 µg/m³.

Extend the compound list

The same hardware can resolve up to 40 hydrocarbons - including octane, chloroethenes and trimethylbenzenes - with suitable settings and calibration gas.

07 · FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is the BTEX analyzer?

An online gas chromatograph that measures benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene and xylenes in ambient or industrial air, with one full cycle every 30 minutes.

Why is BTEX monitoring important?

BTEX compounds are volatile organic pollutants with health and environmental impacts. Benzene in particular is carcinogenic, so speciated measurement supports compliance and public safety.

How does it detect BTEX compounds?

Tenax GR pre-concentration, thermal desorption, capillary-column separation and photo-ionization detection quantify each compound by name.

Where is it used?

Environmental monitoring stations, industrial sites, fuel filling stations, petrochemical facilities, terminals and research laboratories.

Does it support remote data output?

Yes. RS-232, analog and digital I/O, and TCP/IP support reporting to SCADA systems, servers and cloud platforms.

Can it measure compounds beyond BTEX?

Yes. With suitable settings and calibration gas, it can resolve up to 40 hydrocarbons in one run.

How sensitive is it to benzene?

The lower detectable limit is 0.1 µg/m³, equivalent to 0.03 ppbV, with reproducibility under 3% at 1 ppbV.

How is it calibrated?

A standard 4-point calibration covers 4-16 ppbV, while software-driven multipoint validation uses different sample volumes through the trap.

What does it need to run?

Quality-5.0 nitrogen carrier gas at 4 bar and approximately 6 ml/min, plus standard 230 VAC power.

What maintenance does it need?

Preventive maintenance is required once a year. The Tenax trap, capillary column and nitrogen supply are the routine consumables.
