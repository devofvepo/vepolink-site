01 · Overview

A sensor that goes where the worker goes.

A single-gas detector is the simplest layer of a gas safety system: a compact monitor dedicated to one target gas, worn or carried into the area where that gas could be present. It alarms locally so the wearer can leave before exposure becomes dangerous.

An embedded controller, backlit graphical display, two user-set alarm thresholds and a plug-in sensor head share the same impact-resistant housing whichever sensor is fitted.

02 · Sensing principles

Four ways to see a gas.

No single sensor covers every hazard. The detector accepts four sensing technologies, each matched to the chemistry of the gas it is configured to find.

Electrochemical

Toxic gases & oxygen

CO · H₂S · SO₂ · NH₃ · Cl₂ · O₂

Gas diffusing into the cell is oxidised or reduced at the sensing electrode, producing a current in direct proportion to concentration.

Catalytic bead

Combustible gases

CH₄ · LPG · H₂ · hexane · ethylene

Flammable gas burns on a heated catalytic bead. The resistance change relative to an inert reference reads out as percent of lower explosive limit.

NDIR

Carbon dioxide & hydrocarbons

Non-dispersive infrared absorption

An infrared beam crosses the gas cell at a wavelength the target absorbs, measuring concentration without oxygen or sensor poisoning.

PID

Volatile organic compounds

10.6 eV photo-ionisation

Ultraviolet light ionises organic vapours and the resulting ion current screens solvents, fuels and industrial VOCs down to low concentrations.

Step 01

Bump and go

Power on, watch the self-test and confirm sensor response with a short burst of test gas before clipping it on.

Step 02

Sample

Gas reaches the sensor by diffusion, or through the optional suction pump for confined-space pre-entry checks.

Step 03

Alarm

Crossing Low warns; crossing High demands evacuation. Buzzer, LEDs and vibration activate together.

Step 04

Log and review

Readings and alarm events are stored on board and downloaded over USB for exposure and incident records.

05 · Use & upkeep

Clipped on, checked, trusted.

A personal monitor is only as good as its last check. Bump test before every shift, calibrate at scheduled intervals and replace the sensor when response slows.

01 · Wearing

In the breathing zone

Clip it to a collar, lapel or chest pocket with the sensor grille unobstructed.

02 · Bump test

Daily, seconds

A short exposure to test gas confirms the sensor and alarms respond before entry.

03 · Calibration

Zero and span

Zero in clean air and span with a known concentration using the keypad workflow.

04 · Charging

Overnight over USB

A full charge delivers up to 15 hours, with a warning well before shutdown.

05 · Sensor change

User serviceable

The plug-in sensor swaps in minutes and is recalibrated before returning to service.

06 · Records

Downloaded over USB

Logged readings and alarms export to a PC for exposure history and incident review.

07 · FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is the sampling method?

Diffusion suits personal wear; the optional pumped version draws a sample through a probe for pre-entry checks.

Can the user replace the sensor?

Yes. The plug-in sensor module can be replaced by the user and then recalibrated.

Can the user calibrate the unit?

Yes. Zero and span calibration is performed from the keypad with standard calibration gas.

Which gases can be detected?

Oxygen, combustibles, toxic gases, carbon dioxide and VOCs - one configured gas per unit.

How long does a sensor last?

Typically three to four years in normal service. Bump tests and calibration reveal slowing response.

How are alarms set?

Low and High thresholds are set from the keypad and retained in memory. Buzzer, LED and vibration activate together.

How long does the battery last?

Up to 15 hours of continuous operation on a full charge, with charging over USB.

Can readings be downloaded?

Yes. Logged readings and alarm events export over USB for exposure records and incident review.
